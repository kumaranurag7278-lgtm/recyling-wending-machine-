⚠️ Mechanical Engineering Audit

Why 1 Tiny Servo + 3 Narrow Sliders Jams (And The Real Fix)

Analysis of standard beverage containers (500ml bottle: 21cm × 6.5cm, Coke can: 12cm × 6.6cm) vs 9g micro-servo torque.

❌ Why The Generated Image Is Unfeasible

1. Slider Width vs Bottle Size: A 500ml water bottle is 21 cm long. Splitting a 30 cm box into 3 side-by-side ramps gives only 8-9 cm per ramp. When a bottle tilts, it immediately bridges across the walls and gets stuck horizontally.

2. SG90 Torque Limits: An SG90 micro-servo produces only 1.6 kg·cm torque with plastic gears. If a 150g metal can or heavy bottle rests directly on a flap, the servo stalls and strips its gears.

✅ The Only 2 Physically Feasible Solutions

Option A (1 Single Servo): Rotating Chute Pipe (Turntable Funnel): Instead of tilting a heavy flap against an item, the servo rotates a wide cylindrical pipe (or funnel) from above to aim at 3 large ground buckets.

Option B (2 Servos - Standard Academic Way): 2-Stage Cascaded Trapdoors: Flap 1 (Servo 1) decides Plastic vs Rest. If not plastic, it drops to Flap 2 (Servo 2) which decides Metal vs Paper. Wide slides, zero jamming.

Option A: The 1-Servo Rotating Revolver Funnel (100% Jam-Proof)

Recommended for 1 Servo

How Option A Works Mechanically with 1 Servo:

  1. The user drops the bottle/can into the top hole.
  2. Inside, there are no tiny 8cm ramps. Instead, there is 1 wide rotating chute/funnel (12 cm wide).
  3. Before the item drops, the servo rotates the mouth of the chute to point directly into: Left (Plastic), Center (Metal), or Right (Paper).
  4. The item drops by pure gravity straight into that large bin. Zero jams, zero motor overload.
